# Graduate Abstract Algebra: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Proof-based undergraduate algebra including groups, rings, fields, and linear algebra.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Groups and actions.** Group actions, orbit-stabilizer, class equation, Sylow theory, and semidirect products.
2. **Structure of groups.** Normal and composition series, solvability, nilpotence, and finite group examples.
3. **Rings and ideals.** Quotients, localization, polynomial rings, Euclidean domains, PIDs, and UFDs.
4. **Modules.** Exact sequences, free and projective modules, structure over PIDs, and tensor products.
5. **Field extensions.** Algebraic and separable extensions, splitting fields, and primitive element theorem.
6. **Galois theory.** Galois groups, fixed fields, correspondence, solvability by radicals, and explicit examples.
7. **Commutative algebra foundations.** Noetherian rings, prime ideals, localization, integral extensions, and dimension basics.
8. **Homological algebra introduction.** Chain complexes, exactness, derived functor motivation, and Ext/Tor at course-appropriate depth.
9. **Categories and universal constructions.** Groups, rings, modules, products, coproducts, and adjunction motivation.
10. **Algebraic structures in context.** Group and ring representations, symmetry, and links to geometry and number theory.
11. **Selected advanced topics.** Finite fields, algebraic closure, valuation rings, or classical structure theorems.
12. **Synthesis and proof practice.** Hypothesis audits, counterexamples, and alternate proof routes for central theorems.

Record exact editions, chapter or section numbers, stable URLs or catalog metadata, and any access or license restrictions. Keep source excerpts separate from original Meta-Math exposition.

Select projects only after the resource index is in place. Prefer a progression from a foundational theorem to a standard application and then to a graduate-level synthesis. For each selected project, record the exact statement, definitions, hypotheses, supporting imports, a dependency workflow, component-specific node activities, a continuous proof, a boundary test, and an evidence/review status. ### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
