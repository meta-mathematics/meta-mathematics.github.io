# Graduate Representation Theory and Lie Theory: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Linear algebra and abstract algebra; group actions and modules are essential. Some courses assume topology or differential geometry for Lie groups.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Representations and modules.** Group representations, invariant subspaces, irreducibility, equivalence, and Maschke theorem.
2. **Finite-group characters.** Character orthogonality, regular representation, character tables, and decomposition.
3. **Induction and restriction.** Induced representations, Frobenius reciprocity, tensor products, and examples.
4. **Representations of compact groups.** Invariant inner products, Haar integration motivation, and Peter–Weyl overview.
5. **Lie groups and Lie algebras.** Smooth group structures, tangent algebra, exponential map, and the adjoint representation.
6. **Structure of Lie algebras.** Ideals, solvable and nilpotent algebras, semisimple algebras, and Cartan criteria as appropriate.
7. **The sl2 model.** Highest-weight modules, weight strings, and complete finite-dimensional classification.
8. **Root systems and Weyl groups.** Cartan subalgebras, roots, reflections, Dynkin diagrams, and classification framework.
9. **Highest-weight theory.** Dominant weights, Verma modules, finite-dimensional irreducibles, and Weyl character formula.
10. **Representations of Lie groups.** Integration of Lie algebra representations, compact forms, and selected examples.
11. **Connections and applications.** Symmetry in differential equations, geometry, physics, and algebraic combinatorics.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Algebraic groups, reductive groups, Harish-Chandra theory, or geometric representation theory.

### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
