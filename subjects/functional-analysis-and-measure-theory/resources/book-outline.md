# Measure Theory and Functional Analysis: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Real analysis and linear algebra; students should be comfortable with metric spaces and proof-based arguments.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Measures and measurable spaces.** Sigma-algebras, measurable functions, outer measure, extension, and standard examples.
2. **Lebesgue integration.** Simple functions, construction of the integral, monotone and dominated convergence.
3. **Product measures and differentiation.** Tonelli/Fubini, product spaces, absolute continuity, Radon–Nikodym theorem, and differentiation of measures.
4. **Lp spaces.** Inequalities, completeness, duality, convergence, and examples on finite and infinite measure spaces.
5. **Banach and Hilbert spaces.** Completeness, orthogonality, projections, Riesz representation, and geometry of normed spaces.
6. **Bounded operators.** Operator norms, adjoints, inverse operators, and examples from sequence and function spaces.
7. **Core functional-analytic theorems.** Hahn–Banach, uniform boundedness, open mapping, closed graph, and precise hypotheses.
8. **Weak topologies.** Weak and weak-star convergence, compactness, reflexivity, and Banach–Alaoglu.
9. **Compact operators and spectra.** Compactness, spectral theory for compact operators, resolvents, and examples.
10. **Hilbert-space spectral theory.** Self-adjoint and normal operators; spectral theorem in the chosen course level of generality.
11. **Distributions and duality.** Test functions, distributions, weak derivatives, and bridges to Sobolev spaces and PDE.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Banach algebras, operator algebras, ergodic theory, or locally convex spaces.

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
