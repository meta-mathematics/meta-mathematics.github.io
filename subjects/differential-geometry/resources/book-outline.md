# Graduate Differential Geometry: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Advanced calculus, linear algebra, point-set topology, and proof-based real analysis.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Smooth manifolds.** Charts, atlases, smooth maps, submanifolds, and standard examples.
2. **Tangent and cotangent structures.** Tangent spaces, vector fields, covectors, bundles, and differential forms.
3. **Tensor calculus.** Tensor bundles, pullbacks, contractions, and coordinate-independent calculations.
4. **Differential forms and integration.** Exterior derivative, orientation, integration on manifolds, and Stokes theorem.
5. **Vector fields and flows.** Lie brackets, flows, Lie derivative, Frobenius theorem, and distributions.
6. **Riemannian metrics.** Metric tensors, lengths, angles, volume, and induced metrics.
7. **Connections and covariant derivatives.** Affine connections, parallel transport, torsion, and metric compatibility.
8. **Geodesics and completeness.** Geodesic equation, exponential map, normal neighborhoods, and completeness.
9. **Curvature.** Riemann, Ricci, and sectional curvature; symmetries and model spaces.
10. **Submanifold geometry.** Second fundamental form, Gauss and Codazzi equations, and examples.
11. **Global theorems.** Gauss–Bonnet, comparison ideas, and links to topology.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Lie groups, symplectic geometry, gauge theory, geometric analysis, or general relativity.

### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
