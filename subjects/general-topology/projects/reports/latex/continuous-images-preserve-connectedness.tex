\noindent\textbf{GT-01 / General Topology / Report v1}

\section{Target and import contract}

\begin{theorem}

For a connected topological space \(X\) and continuous \(f:X\to Y\),
prove \(f(X)\) is connected in the subspace topology. Deduce the
Intermediate Value Theorem for continuous maps from real intervals to
\(\mathbb R\).

\end{theorem}

\subsection{Foundations and permitted imports}

A separation of a space is a pair of disjoint nonempty open subsets
covering it; connected means there is no separation. Use continuity as
openness of inverse images and the subspace topology. Import
connectedness of real intervals, as a least-upper-bound consequence; no
statement about continuity of an inverse is involved.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Numbered mathematical activities}

\begin{enumerate}[label=N1.\arabic*,leftmargin=3.4em]

\item Suppose \(f(X)=U\cup V\) is a separation in its subspace topology.

\item Write \(U=f(X)\cap O\) and \(V=f(X)\cap P\) for open \(O,P\subset Y\).

\item Their inverse images are open in X by continuity.

\item Both are nonempty because U,V are nonempty subsets of the image.

\item They are disjoint and coverX.

\item This contradicts connectedness.

\item Therefore \(f(X)\) is connected; the empty image, if X is empty, also
has no separation.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Worked orchestration}

The continuous map \(f(t)=(cost,sint)\) \(from[0,2\pi ]\) has image
\(S^{1}\), so the circle is connected. For \(f(t)=t^{2}\) \(on[-1,2]\),
the image{[}0,4{]} contains every value between the selected endpoint
values 1 and 4, even though the map is not injective.

\item
  \textbf{Action:} redo the affected mathematical activity using the

  feeding their conclusions back as premises.
\item
  \textbf{Ego:} publish only the supported export with its assumptions
  and proof location. A hypothetical revised export is v2; the written
  proof here is the v1 exposition, not a claim that this repair was
  executed.
\end{enumerate}
