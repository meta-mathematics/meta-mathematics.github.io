# Graduate Probability and Stochastic Processes: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Measure theory, integration, real analysis, and elementary linear algebra.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Probability spaces and random variables.** Measurability, distributions, expectation, independence, and examples.
2. **Modes of convergence.** Almost sure, in probability, in Lp, and in distribution; implications and counterexamples.
3. **Limit theorems.** Borel–Cantelli, strong and weak laws, characteristic functions, and central limit theorem.
4. **Conditional expectation.** Radon–Nikodym construction, conditioning, filtrations, and martingale viewpoint.
5. **Martingales.** Stopping times, optional stopping conditions, convergence theorems, and inequalities.
6. **Markov chains.** Transition kernels, recurrence, invariant measures, and convergence.
7. **Poisson processes.** Counting processes, interarrival structure, thinning, and superposition.
8. **Brownian motion.** Construction properties, paths, Gaussian increments, and scaling.
9. **Stochastic integration.** Itô integral, isometry, Itô formula, and stochastic differential equations.
10. **Diffusions and generators.** Kolmogorov equations, generators, martingale problems, and boundary behavior.
11. **Stationary processes.** Autocorrelation, spectral viewpoint, ergodic theorem, and selected applications.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Large deviations, stochastic calculus for finance, interacting systems, or random graphs.
