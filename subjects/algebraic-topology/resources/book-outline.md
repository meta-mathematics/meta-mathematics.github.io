# Graduate Algebraic Topology: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

General topology, group theory, and comfort with chain complexes; point-set topology and homological algebra are helpful.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Homotopy and fundamental group.** Homotopies, loops, fundamental group, van Kampen theorem, and covering spaces.
2. **Singular homology.** Chains, boundaries, homology groups, functoriality, and homotopy invariance.
3. **Exact sequences.** Long exact sequence of a pair, excision, Mayer–Vietoris, and computation strategy.
4. **CW complexes.** Cellular homology, attaching maps, skeleta, and cellular approximation.
5. **Cohomology.** Cochains, cup products, ring structure, and universal coefficient ideas.
6. **Homology theories and axioms.** Eilenberg–Steenrod principles and comparison of singular and cellular theories.
7. **Homotopy groups.** Higher homotopy, Hurewicz theorem, Whitehead theorem, and selected computations.
8. **Bundles and characteristic classes.** Vector bundles, orientations, Euler and Chern classes, and classifying-space motivation.
9. **Duality.** Poincaré duality, manifolds, cap product, and intersection pairing.
10. **Spectral sequences.** Filtered complexes, construction, convergence conditions, and a guided computation.
11. **K-theory or stable homotopy.** Introductory module according to course focus.
12. **Applications and capstone.** Compute invariants, detect non-equivalence, or audit a proof using exact sequences.

Record exact editions, chapter or section numbers, stable URLs or catalog metadata, and any access or license restrictions. Keep source excerpts separate from original Meta-Math exposition.

## Future Meta-Math project pathway

Select projects only after the resource index is in place. Prefer a progression from a foundational theorem to a standard application and then to a graduate-level synthesis. For each selected project, record the exact statement, definitions, hypotheses, supporting imports, a dependency workflow, component-specific node activities, a continuous proof, a boundary test, and an evidence/review status. Avoid implying formal verification unless a proof assistant actually checks the stated theorem.

### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
