\section{Target and import contract}

\begin{theorem}

For every \(n\ge1\) and continuous \(f:D^n\to D^n\), prove \(f\) has a
fixed point by deriving a forbidden retraction \(D^n\to S^{n-1}\) from a
fixed-point-free map.

\end{theorem}

\subsection{Foundations and permitted imports}

\(D^{n}\) is the closed Euclidean unit ball and \(S^{(n-1)}\) its
boundary, \(n\ge 1\). Import homotopy invariance of reduced singular
homology, functoriality, contractibility of \(D^{n}\), and the sphere
computation \(\widetilde H_{n-1}(S^{(n-1)}\);\(Z)=Z\). For \(n=1\) this
is reduced \(H_{0}(S^{0})=Z\), while reduced \(H_{0}(D^{1})=0\). The
no-retraction result is proved below.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Numbered mathematical activities}

\begin{enumerate}[label=N1.\arabic*,leftmargin=3.4em]

\item Put \(v=x-f(x)\ne 0\).

\item The positive endpoint of the ray \(y=f(x)+tv\) on the unit sphere is
obtained at
\(t(x)=[-f(x)\cdot v+\sqrt{((f(x)\cdot v)^{2}}+(1-\Vert f(x)\Vert ^{2})\Vert v\Vert ^{2})]/\Vert v\Vert ^{2}\).

\item The denominator is nonzero.

\item This is the larger root of \(\Vert f(x)+tv\Vert ^{2}=1\), and \(t\ge 1\)
because x at \(t=1\) lies in the ball.

\item If \(f(x)\) lies on the sphere then \(f(x)\cdot v<0\) unless \(x=f(x)\),
so the chosen root is still positive.

\item The displayed expression is continuous, and \(r(x)=f(x)+t(x)v\) is
sphere-valued.

\item Ifx is on the sphere, \(t=1\): the ball's ray intersection ends there,
since distinct points of a Euclidean sphere have a strictly interior
connecting segment.

\item Thus \(r(x)=x\) on the boundary, giving a retraction.

\end{enumerate}

\item A retraction would satisfy \(r\circ i=\operatorname{id} _S\).

\item Hence \(r_*\circ i_*=\operatorname{id}\) on

\section{Worked orchestration}

The constant map \(f(x)=a\) with \(\Vert a\Vert \le 1\) fixes \(x=a\).
The affine \(contractionf(x)=x/2\) also fixes 0. In either example the
hypothetical retraction formula fails at its fixed point because
\(v=0\), exactly the point the contradiction proves must exist.

\subsection{Boundary and hypothesis audit}

A rotation of \(S^{1}\) by a nonzero angle has no fixed point, so a
sphere self-map does not satisfy the disk theorem. The corresponding
rotation of \(D^{2}\) does fix 0. Sense retains both the disk domain and
self-map condition rather than extending the claim to arbitrary compact
spaces.
