# Graduate Harmonic Analysis: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Measure-theoretic real analysis, Lebesgue integration, Lp spaces, and elementary Fourier series.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Fourier series.** Orthogonality, convergence modes, Fejér kernels, and summability.
2. **Fourier transform.** Schwartz functions, inversion, Plancherel theorem, and extension to L2.
3. **Convolution and kernels.** Approximate identities, translation continuity, and smoothing.
4. **Lp inequalities.** Young inequality, Hausdorff–Young inequality, and interpolation principles.
5. **Maximal functions.** Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator, covering lemmas, and differentiation consequences.
6. **Singular integrals.** Principal values, Calderón–Zygmund kernels, cancellation, and Lp boundedness.
7. **Littlewood–Paley ideas.** Frequency localization, square functions, and dyadic decompositions.
8. **Restriction and oscillatory phenomena.** Fourier restriction, oscillatory integrals, and selected estimates.
9. **Harmonic analysis on groups.** Characters and Fourier analysis on compact or locally compact groups, at introductory depth.
10. **Sobolev and potential theory.** Riesz potentials, Sobolev inequalities, and links to elliptic equations.
11. **Applications.** Uniqueness of Fourier transforms, PDE estimates, signal decomposition, or analytic number theory examples.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Wavelets, dispersive estimates, time-frequency analysis, or modern restriction theory.

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
