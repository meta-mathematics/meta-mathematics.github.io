# Graduate Algebraic Geometry: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Commutative algebra, field theory, modules, and basic topology; schemes require greater algebraic maturity.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Affine algebraic sets.** Polynomial zero loci, Zariski topology, and coordinate rings.
2. **Nullstellensatz and correspondences.** Algebraic sets and radical ideals; maps of varieties and ring homomorphisms.
3. **Projective geometry.** Projective space, homogeneous ideals, projective varieties, and elimination.
4. **Dimension and local structure.** Noetherian rings, prime chains, localization, tangent spaces, and dimension.
5. **Morphisms and fibers.** Regular maps, rational maps, dominant maps, finite maps, and fiber behavior.
6. **Curves and divisors.** Smooth curves, divisors, line bundles, differentials, and Riemann–Roch.
7. **Sheaves and cohomology.** Structure sheaf, coherent sheaves, Čech cohomology, and selected vanishing ideas.
8. **Schemes.** Prime spectrum, structure sheaf, affine schemes, and gluing.
9. **Scheme morphisms.** Separatedness, finite type, properness, fiber products, and base change at an introductory level.
10. **Smoothness and singularities.** Jacobian criterion, regular local rings, and examples of singular spaces.
11. **Intersection and birational themes.** Divisors, intersection numbers, blowups, and birational maps in selected examples.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Étale morphisms, moduli, arithmetic geometry, or cohomological methods.

### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
