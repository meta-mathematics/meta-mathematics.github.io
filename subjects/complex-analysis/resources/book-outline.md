# Graduate Complex Analysis: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Real analysis, elementary complex variables, and basic topology; measure theory helps with boundary-value topics.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Holomorphic functions.** Complex differentiability, Cauchy–Riemann equations, power series, and analytic continuation.
2. **Cauchy theory.** Cauchy integral theorem and formula, winding number, and deformation of contours.
3. **Zeros and singularities.** Identity theorem, zero counting, isolated singularities, Laurent series, and classification of singularities.
4. **Residues and applications.** Residue theorem, argument principle, Rouché theorem, and contour integral evaluation.
5. **Conformal maps.** Möbius transformations, normalizations, Schwarz lemma, and mapping geometry.
6. **Maximum principles.** Maximum modulus, open mapping, Schwarz reflection, and harmonic conjugates.
7. **Normal families.** Montel theorem, compact convergence, Hurwitz theorem, and compactness methods.
8. **Riemann mapping.** Extremal families, normal limits, injectivity, surjectivity, and uniqueness under normalization.
9. **Entire and meromorphic functions.** Growth, Jensen formula, products and factorization in selected depth.
10. **Harmonic functions and boundary values.** Poisson kernel, Dirichlet problem on standard domains, and boundary convergence.
11. **Elliptic functions and Riemann surfaces.** Lattices, doubly periodic functions, analytic continuation, and introductory surface concepts.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Hardy spaces, several complex variables, potential theory, or geometric function theory.

Select projects only after the resource index is in place. Prefer a progression from a foundational theorem to a standard application and then to a graduate-level synthesis. ### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
