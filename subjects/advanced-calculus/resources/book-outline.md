# Advanced Calculus and Multivariable Analysis: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Linear algebra, single-variable real analysis, multivariable calculus, and proof-writing.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Normed spaces and differentiability.** Norms, limits, continuity, Fréchet derivatives, and the relation to coordinate partial derivatives.
2. **Linear approximation.** Derivative as a linear map, chain rule, mean-value estimates, and remainder bounds.
3. **Inverse and implicit function theorems.** Local invertibility, regular level sets, coordinate changes, and hypothesis checks.
4. **Taylor theory.** Higher derivatives, multilinear forms, Taylor expansions, and error terms.
5. **Integration in several variables.** Riemann and Lebesgue perspectives, iterated integrals, and Fubini/Tonelli prerequisites.
6. **Change of variables.** Jacobian determinant, orientation, substitutions, and geometric meaning of volume scaling.
7. **Vector calculus.** Divergence, curl, flux, circulation, and integral identities.
8. **Differential forms.** Exterior algebra, pullbacks, exterior derivative, and coordinate invariance.
9. **Green, Gauss, and Stokes.** Generalized Stokes theorem, boundary orientation, and classical integral theorems as instances.
10. **Constrained extrema.** Lagrange multipliers, regular constraints, and second-derivative tests.
11. **Parameter-dependent integrals.** Differentiation under the integral sign and the domination or uniformity hypotheses required.
12. **Optional bridges.** Inverse function theorem applications, local coordinates on manifolds, and rigorous numerical error estimates.

## Future Meta-Math project pathway

Select projects only after the resource index is in place. Prefer a progression from a foundational theorem to a standard application and then to a graduate-level synthesis. For each selected project, record the exact statement, definitions, hypotheses, supporting imports, a dependency workflow, faculty-specific node activities, a continuous proof, a boundary test, and an evidence/review status. ### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
