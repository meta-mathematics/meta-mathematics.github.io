# Advanced Linear Algebra: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Proof-based undergraduate linear algebra, polynomial algebra, and basic abstract algebra.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Vector spaces and operators.** Invariant subspaces, quotient spaces, duality, and operator representation.
2. **Polynomial methods.** Minimal and characteristic polynomials, Cayley–Hamilton, and primary decomposition.
3. **Canonical forms.** Rational canonical form, Jordan form, and invariant factors.
4. **Modules over a PID.** Linear operators as module actions and the structure theorem for finitely generated modules.
5. **Bilinear and quadratic forms.** Orthogonality, congruence, classification over selected fields, and Witt ideas.
6. **Inner-product spaces.** Adjoints, normal operators, orthogonal and unitary diagonalization.
7. **Spectral theory in finite dimensions.** Real symmetric and complex Hermitian operators, Rayleigh quotients, and variational characterization.
8. **Tensor and exterior algebra.** Tensor products, symmetric and exterior powers, determinants, and multilinear forms.
9. **Matrix analysis.** Positive operators, singular values, polar decomposition, and matrix inequalities.
10. **Numerical and perturbation viewpoints.** Conditioning, eigenvalue perturbation, and stable factorizations.
11. **Applications.** Least squares, differential equations, graph spectra, and quantum-mechanical observables.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Infinite-dimensional operators, representation connections, or algebraic K-theory motivation.
