# Graduate Number Theory: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Proof-based undergraduate algebra and number theory; elementary group and ring theory; basic complex analysis is useful for analytic topics.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Arithmetic foundations.** Divisibility, gcd and Bézout identities, congruences, Chinese remainder theorem, and arithmetic functions.
2. **Multiplicative structure.** Units, primitive roots, orders modulo n, cyclic groups of units, and the structure of finite fields.
3. **Quadratic residues.** Legendre and Jacobi symbols, quadratic reciprocity, Gauss sums, and representation by quadratic forms.
4. **Congruence methods.** Fermat and Euler theorems, lifting roots, Hensel lemma, and p-adic viewpoints.
5. **Prime distribution.** Chebyshev estimates, Dirichlet characters, Dirichlet theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, and analytic tools behind it.
6. **Diophantine equations.** Pythagorean triples, Pell equations, local obstructions, descent, and selected methods for integral points.
7. **Algebraic number fields.** Number rings, integral elements, discriminants, embeddings, norms, and traces.
8. **Ideals and class groups.** Dedekind domains, unique factorization of ideals, ideal class groups, and examples of failure of unique factorization for elements.
9. **Units and extensions.** Dirichlet unit theorem, ramification, decomposition of primes, and splitting in extensions.
10. **Zeta and L-functions.** Dedekind zeta functions, Euler products, analytic continuation in selected cases, and the prime ideal theorem as an advanced endpoint.
11. **Optional advanced modules.** Local fields, p-adic numbers, elliptic curves, modular forms, and a selected modern theorem or conjecture.

## Future Meta-Math project pathway

Select projects only after the resource index is in place. Prefer a progression from a foundational theorem to a standard application and then to a graduate-level synthesis. For each selected project, record the exact statement, definitions, hypotheses, supporting imports, a dependency workflow, faculty-specific node activities, a continuous proof, a boundary test, and an evidence/review status. ### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
