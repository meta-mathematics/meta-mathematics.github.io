# Graduate Real Analysis: Graduate Course and Book Outline

> This is an original, graduate-level syllabus map synthesized from standard course coverage. It is a planning document, not a transcription of any one textbook table of contents. Topic order and depth vary by program and instructor.

## Audience and prerequisites

Proof-based calculus, metric spaces, and elementary linear algebra; measure theory is introduced from first principles.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a student should be able to:
- state the central definitions and explain the role of their hypotheses;
- prove and apply the core theorems in the syllabus, identifying which results are imported;
- construct examples and counterexamples that test the scope of a claim;
- connect the subject to at least one neighboring area without treating an analogy as a proof.

## Proposed chapter sequence

1. **Metric and topological preliminaries.** Completeness, compactness, continuity, and uniform convergence.
2. **Measure construction.** Outer measure, measurable sets, Lebesgue measure, and completion.
3. **Measurable functions and integration.** Simple approximation, Lebesgue integral, and comparison with Riemann integration.
4. **Convergence theorems.** Monotone, Fatou, dominated convergence; almost-everywhere and in-measure convergence.
5. **Lp spaces and inequalities.** Hölder, Minkowski, completeness, approximation, and basic duality.
6. **Product integration.** Product measures, Fubini and Tonelli, and parameterized integrals.
7. **Signed and complex measures.** Jordan decomposition, total variation, Radon–Nikodym theorem, and absolute continuity.
8. **Differentiation and covering.** Lebesgue differentiation theorem, maximal estimates, and differentiation of integrals.
9. **Functions of bounded variation.** Variation, absolute continuity, differentiation, and the fundamental theorem for Lebesgue integrals.
10. **Sequences of functions.** Uniform integrability, Egorov and Lusin theorems, and compactness themes.
11. **Applications.** Probability foundations, Fourier analysis, and weak formulations of differential equations.
12. **Optional advanced modules.** Geometric measure theory, Banach-valued integration, or ergodic theory.

### Scope notes

Treat this syllabus as a flexible map. An instructor may omit or replace advanced units, and some programs teach a topic as a separate course. Before building a book index, note which chapters are core, optional, prerequisites, or deferred; cross-link overlapping material rather than duplicating it.
